# The Game Is Part of the Story: Turning Star Citizen's Limits into Roleplay

Star Citizen is an alpha in 2956 (real 2026, Alpha 4.10). Servers crash, ships desync, elevators break, and NPCs
can't hold a conversation. Instead of breaking immersion, the DM treats these as **in-universe events**.
Every rule below is optional table-talk that the crew can lean into.

| What happens in the game | What it is in the 'Verse | How to roleplay it |
|---|---|---|
| **30k / server crash / disconnect** | A **comms blackout**: quantum interference, a sensor storm, or someone jamming the area. | Whoever dropped was "pulled off-grid". When they're back, they say where they were. The crew covers for them in the meantime. |
| **Death → wake up in a med bed** | **Regen.** Your imprint was restored, but every regen costs Imprint Viability (canon: the Regen Crisis, trauma echoes). | Each death leaves an echo, a small quirk or memory gap you invent. A third death in one mission leaves a lasting scar on your character sheet. |
| **Desync, rubber-banding, ships teleporting** | **Sensor spoofing and jamming.** The enemy has good ECM, or the area is a quantum mess. | Call it out in character: "They're spoofing our scanners!" Treat it as the antagonist's tech. |
| **Freight elevator won't work / cargo stuck** | A **customs hold, dockworker strike, or tampering.** | Someone has to "talk to the dockmaster". RP a bribe or an argument, try another terminal or station, and suspect sabotage. |
| **Items are stored per station (item banks)** | Gear is **physical**. It's in a locker where you left it. | Logistics are part of the job: supply runs, hidden caches, "my rifle's in a locker in Lorville". |
| **Ship destroyed → insurance claim timer** | **Insurance bureaucracy**, plus suspicion of fraud. | The ship is "in the shop". Grumble at the insurer and borrow a crewmate's ship. Fly a loaner and owe someone a favour. |
| **CrimeStat** | An **active warrant.** Landing rights are revoked and turrets go hot. | You're a fugitive: hide at GrimHEX or Ruin Station, pay off the fine (a bribe), or have the crew smuggle you in. |
| **NPCs can't talk; missions come from the contract manager** | Contacts talk through **encrypted comms**: the DM and the players voice them. Contracts are **cover jobs**. | Any matching contract counts as the story job. The mobiGlas listing is the cover; the real job is what the DM said. |
| **Mission target didn't spawn / objective bugged** | **Bad intel**, or **someone got there first**. | That's a valid outcome. Report it, and the DM folds it into the twist. |
| **Long quantum travel** | **Transit time.** | The natural time for in-character talk: planning, arguing, telling stories. No scene-setting needed. |
| **Hangar / ship elevator delays** | **The deck crew is slow.** Tips speed them up. | Banter with the deck chief, played by a crewmate. |
| **Dumb NPC AI, T-posing guards** | **Cheap hired guns** on bargain-bin stims. | Mock them. Then be careful, because cheap guns still kill. |
| **Other players show up** | **Part of the story**: rival crews, witnesses, the antagonist's muscle. | If a random player attacks you, they were hired. If they help, they're a contact now. |
| **Empty server / nobody around** | **The lanes are quiet.** Too quiet. | Tension. Someone cleared the area for a reason. |
| **Wipes / persistence resets** | A **market crash or asset seizure**: the UEE or the corps froze accounts. | Your character's story survives; their wallet didn't. Start the comeback arc. |

## How missions use this

- Missions don't set scenes or script dialogue. They give a **situation**, **stakes**, **how the crew's stories cross**,
  and **in-game objectives** that any matching contract or activity satisfies.
- Each mission includes the two or three rules most likely to come up (for example, freight rules for a smuggling run).
- **Failure counts as story.** A 30k, a bugged objective or a death spiral is reported as what happened, and the DM builds on it.
